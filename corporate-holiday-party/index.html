How karaoke runs
Built for a work crowd

Nobody gets put on the spot.

  • Voluntary by design. Watching is a way to play, and the host says so out loud.
  • Teams, not solo contestants. The table plays together, so nobody stands alone.
  • Short rounds. Nobody is trapped in a game while their food gets cold.
  • Winter theme, work-clean content. Every question is written to include the January hires.
  • Your year in the questions. We write rounds about your company from a short survey before the event, without inside jokes that exclude anyone.
  • The giveaway is the game. The prize wheel hands out whatever your team brought, and that spin is the moment people wait for.

Questions people ask

The honest answers.

Our party is during the workday. Is that a thing?

Yes. Most company parties are now, and weekday and daytime dates are open. The show plays the same at noon in a conference room as it does at night in a hall.

How big a crowd can play?

The formats play in teams, so the room decides the size: a conference room plays as two tables, a function hall plays as departments. Everyone else plays along from their seat.

Do people have to participate?

No. That is the first thing the host says. The rounds are short, the teams are voluntary, and the people who just watch still get the show.

What does the room need?

A screen or TV we can plug into and an outlet. We bring the sound, the buzzers, the board, and the host. Prizes are yours to supply, and the wheel can hand them out.
