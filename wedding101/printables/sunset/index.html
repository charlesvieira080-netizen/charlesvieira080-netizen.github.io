  1. Between two rows, split the difference: sunset moves about a minute a day. Check the exact minute for your venue and date before you print the run of show.
  2. Golden hour starts about an hour before sunset. Last light is the end of civil twilight, when the sky stops helping the camera.
  3. With no first look, the ceremony should end about two hours before sunset: the family photos and the portraits happen in daylight, and the cocktail hour covers them.
  4. With a first look, most of the portraits are done before the ceremony, and an hour before sunset is enough.
  5. An outdoor ceremony should end no later than thirty minutes before sunset, or the recessional walks out in the dark.
  6. Sunset portraits are a fifteen-minute step-out at thirty minutes before sunset. Put it in the run of show so the MC and the caterer know where you went.
  7. The clocks change in March and November. On the weekend of the change, sunset jumps an hour; the table shows the clock in force for each date.
