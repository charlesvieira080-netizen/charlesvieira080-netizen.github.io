The rotation

Four weeks, four different games.

Week one

The Quiz Show

Read aloud, with a wireless buzzer on every table. Categories written for the room: the town, the decades, the music. The board goes up on your screen and the host keeps score, so staff get to play too.

How the Quiz Show plays

Week two: Music Bingo

We play a clip, the room marks the title. Large-print cards, oldies the room grew up on, and nobody races anybody. It ends on a song everyone sings.

How music bingo plays

Week three: The Survey Showdown

The top answers hidden on the board, played one guess at a time. Everybody has an opinion, which is the whole point.

How the Showdown plays

Week four: host's choice

The sing-along with the words in big type on your screen, Spin and Sing, Name That Tune, or a music hour from the decades your room danced to. Eight programs in the library means the calendar never repeats itself into the ground.

Why weekly beats one-off

A program, not a booking.

  • The host learns the room. Names, the regulars, who wants the mic brought to the seat and who would rather listen. That takes more than one visit.
  • The calendar fills itself. A standing slot is one decision instead of twelve. Your activity calendar gets a weekly anchor residents recognize on sight.
  • The games rotate, so week four feels as fresh as week one. Eight programs, one host, zero setup on your end.
  • Everything arrives with us. Sound, mics, the screen feed and the questions. Your room stays your room.
  • Clean content, front-row safe. Every question is read before the visit.
Who books a weekly slot

Rooms with regulars.

  • Senior living communities, weekday mornings and afternoons at the published daytime rate.
  • Senior centers and councils on aging, a weekday program the whole town can come to.
  • Adult day programs, hands-up formats and large-print cards built for the room.
  • Clubs, lodges and veterans posts, a standing night members bring friends to.
  • Parish halls and libraries, trivia or a sing-along as recurring community programming.
The honest answers

What directors ask us first.

What does a weekly program cost?

The single daytime visit is $200, published on the senior living page, and it covers the host, the sound, the screen feed, setup and pack-up. Weekly and evening arrangements are quoted in writing for your room and your schedule, so the number you plan around is the number you pay.

Do we need any equipment?

No. We bring the sound, the mics and the feed for your room's screen, and we set up and pack up around your schedule.

Can residents with low vision or low mobility play?

Yes. The Quiz Show is read aloud with a buzzer on every table, music bingo uses large-print cards, and the sing-along puts the words in big type on the screen with a wireless mic that comes to the seat.

What if we want to try it first?

Book one daytime visit at the published rate and watch your room play. If the slot earns its place on the calendar, we make it weekly.
