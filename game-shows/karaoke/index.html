How it runs

A queue, a mic, and a merciful host.

  • The rule, before the first song: sign up once, one song per turn, first-timers go up early, and the host calls the names.
  • A group song opens it, then a confident singer, never the hesitant one.
  • A duet, a chorus, singing from the seat, or cheering: nobody has to sing, and the pass costs nothing.
  • One invitation for the shy, offered once, and a graceful pass if the answer is no.
  • Music between singers, so the room never waits and the floor never goes quiet.
With the DJ package

Same booth, same sound.

Karaoke runs from the same booth and the same sound as the DJ set, with no pauses. It lands after dinner and the formal dances as a placed block, an hour, or the whole party, and when the floor is packed, the songbook waits.

Who sings

Anyone who puts a name in.

Anyone who puts a name in, one song at a time. First-timers go up early, so everyone sings once before anyone sings twice, and the rest of the room is the chorus, which counts.

How it fits your night

A block, an hour, or the whole night.

As a segment inside a DJ night, an hour of it, or the whole party. The host decides when the room is done singing and wants to dance, and the dance floor is already open when it does.

Best for

Rooms that want to be the show.

  • Company holiday parties. Opt-in only: late, after the program, the microphone offered and never assigned, and watching is welcome.
  • Birthdays and milestone parties. The guest of honor's song first, then the room.
  • Rehearsal dinners and after-parties. A short, warm set after the toasts, or a loose late set after the reception, when the formal part is over.

Questions people ask

The honest answers.

Does anyone have to sing?

No. Cheer, sing from your seat, take a chorus, or watch. The pass costs nothing, and the host only asks once.

What stops one person from hogging the mic?

The rule, said out loud before the first song: one song per turn, and the list comes back around.

Can it run alongside the dancing?

Yes, in blocks. Same booth, same sound, music between singers, and when the floor is packed the songbook waits.
