Prioritizing Your Wedding Budget

Think about your priorities and what you really want from your reception, and craft your budget accordingly.

How much time do you have? I could go on and on about wedding budgets, but I'm going to keep this short because there is one concept I want to drive home. How do you decide what to fund first? You decide what you and your partner would miss most if it didn't show up, you fund that first, and you let the rest of the split follow from there. Not the other way around. Most couples build the budget from a list of categories somebody else wrote, then wonder why the thing they cared about got what was left over.

I have worked more weddings than I can count. One that stands out is the wedding where the cigar roller did not show up (yes, cigar rollers were a thing at weddings for a while, and for some families they still are). The night went on without a hitch. People danced, the sendoff was a blast, and everyone still remembers that wedding fondly. At no point did anyone say, 'I wish the cigar roller had shown up, the wedding would have been so much better.' No one smoked a hand-rolled cigar that night, and no one noticed there was supposed to be one.

The removal test

Now try that with everything else. Picture your wedding with the flowers removed. With the photographer removed. With the music removed, the food, the cake, the lights, the bar, the favors. Some of those pictures are a little sadder. Some of them are a different day. When you take that approach, it becomes obvious which providers play the biggest roles in making your wedding memorable, and the answer is different for every couple. That is the point. Write down the three you would notice missing. Those three go to the top of the list, and they get funded first.

Priorities before numbers

When you start the budget, ask yourselves what you want. What parts of the day matter most to the two of you? Maybe it's the food, and you set more aside for a chef you trust. Maybe it's the photos. Maybe it's the room, or the band. Say it out loud, together, before a single deposit goes down, because the order you book things in has a way of becoming the order you fund them in. If you ask me what I would protect first, I'll tell you: whatever keeps the room informed, involved, and entertained, because that is what guests remember for years to come. But you didn't ask me. Ask each other.

Three rules from experience

  • Leave a cushion. Something will come up that nobody budgeted for: an extra hour, a rental you didn't know you needed, a guest count that grew. A slice held back at the start is the difference between an easy yes in the last week and a fight.
  • The guest count is the multiplier. Every name on the list touches food, bar, rentals, invitations, and favors. A shorter list is the only budget move that helps everywhere at once, and it is the one couples avoid the longest.
  • Don't pay for the thing nobody will notice missing. The cigar roller. The extra. The upgrade that looked great in a photo of somebody else's wedding. If it fails the removal test, it goes to the bottom, and the bottom is allowed to fall off.

What usually goes wrong here

The venue gets booked first, at the top of the range, and then eleven other categories get to share what is left. The couple who wanted great photos ends up shopping for a photographer by price. Book in the order of your priorities, and give the top three their share before the venue decides it for you.

No matter what your budget is, list your priorities, and then mold your budget from there. If a fun and memorable reception is high on the list, fund the things that make one, and then do some thorough research. The next four articles are that research.
