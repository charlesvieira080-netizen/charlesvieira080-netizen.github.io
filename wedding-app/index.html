The Wedding App

Your guests stop asking, so you stop answering.

What time, where do I park, is there a shuttle, where is everyone staying. Every wedding produces the same two hundred questions, and they all arrive in the last week, by text, to you. Chapel takes them.

What your guests get

One link, and they are in.

There is nothing to download and no account to make. Your guests tap a link and the whole wedding opens in their phone's browser, with their own name on it and only the parts that apply to them.

Will you be there

RSVP, meal choice, plus one, shuttle seat. It arrives as one clean list instead of forty replies scattered through your text history and your mother's inbox.

The day

The run of show, in order, in plain language. Guests can see what is happening next without asking anyone, which is the whole point.

Request a song

Guests send requests from their seats and they reach the DJ, who is us. More on that below.

The photo wall

Every guest's camera roll, pooled in one place as the night happens. You wake up to the wedding from two hundred angles you were not standing at.

Stay, shuttle and getting there

Hotels, the shuttle, parking, the drive, and what the weather is doing at the venue. Your out of town guests answer their own questions at 11pm the night before.

Somebody to ask

Questions get answered inside the app from what you and your coordinator put in it. When the answer is not in there, it says so and passes the question along rather than guessing.

The part that is ours

The requests come straight to the booth.

Most wedding apps stop at information. This one is run by the company playing your reception, which changes what it can do.

No more napkins

For forty years a request has meant a shouted title or an unreadable napkin, handed over while we are mixing. In the app a guest types it once and it lands on our screen with their name on it.

We see the room before we are in it

Requests start arriving days ahead, so by load-in we know what this crowd wants. No planning form has ever told us that much.

You still decide

A request is a request, not a promise. Your do not play list still wins, and nothing goes on because a guest typed it in.

How it attaches

We build it. You look at it once.

We set it up

Your timeline, venue and hotels come from what you already sent us. You are not filling in another portal.

You approve it

You read it once and tell us what is wrong. Anything we are unsure of stays out rather than going in as a guess.

It goes out with the invitations

A code on the invitation, or a link your people can text around. This is why we ask about it early: it has to be printed before the invitations are.
